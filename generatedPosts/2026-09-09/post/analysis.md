# How this post uses competitors for traction

Source: [tiktok.com/@fashion.technically/video/7665104701272132878](https://www.tiktok.com/@fashion.technically/video/7665104701272132878) (short link `https://www.tiktok.com/t/ZTUDXaRvQ/`)

Not a Lumysi-owned video. It is a 4-slide rec from `@fashion.technically` (~223 followers) that did **605k plays / 36.4k likes / 14.4k saves**. Lumysi is the last product in someone else's roundup — that is the traction mechanic.

## What the slides actually do

1. **Hook names the category king, not Lumysi.** Overlay: “Health Trackers I'd wear instead of **Oura Ring**” plus a credibility kicker (“as a woman who tracks emerging fashion tech for a living”). Oura is the search term. Anyone comparing rings, hate-watching Oura, or shopping WHOOP-class wearables stops.
2. **Two peer competitors first, Lumysi last.** Slide 2 is Lumia Health (ear cuff, $249). Slide 3 is Incora Health (earrings, $250). Slide 4 is Lumysi Bracelets ($179 on Kickstarter). Recency bias plus a cheaper Kickstarter price makes Lumysi the “winner slot” without a hard sell.
3. **Caption tags every brand.** `@wearlumia` `@incorahealth` `@Lumysi` plus “Which is your fav?” That notifies each brand’s audience, invites a comment war, and turns competitor fandom into distribution.
4. **Category hashtags, not brand hashtags.** `#fashiontech #wearabletech #healthtracker` ride Oura/WHOOP discovery, not Lumysi’s own name.
5. **Silent slideshow + original sound.** No VO. The template is on-screen type: brand pill, one-liner, price, optional personal note. Product photography does the rest.

Lumysi does not attack Oura in this clip. It **borrows Oura’s demand**, stands next to two fashion-tech peers so it looks like a real category, then takes the last slide and the lowest price.
